# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Accessibility professionals working in the arts: ASL interpreters, captioners, audio describers, educators, content creators, performers, event/production staff. Also accessible businesses and arts organizations looking to connect with those professionals. They know the field, they've seen every clunky "accessibility" nonprofit page, and they will immediately clock whether this place was built by someone who gets it or someone who means well.

## Product Purpose

A member directory and community platform for arts accessibility professionals. Not a generic networking tool — a home base for a specific, underserved community. Success looks like: a captioner finds a gig, an interpreter finds a community, a theater connects with a CDI, a newcomer realizes this field exists and wants in.

## Brand Personality

Playful, bold, expert. Fun and a little weird — not your average nonprofit. Has strong opinions. Knows the field deeply. Celebrates the work without making it clinical or charitable. The vibe is: "the cool kids' table, but the cool kids are accessibility nerds who also care deeply about equity."

## Anti-references

- Generic nonprofit / charity aesthetics: stock-photo hands, inspiration porn, beige, feel-good vagueness
- Overly corporate / sterile: LinkedIn aesthetic, enterprise SaaS grid, committee-designed blandness
- Disability-as-medical-topic: clinical imagery, medicalized language, accessibility as compliance checkbox
- Trying too hard to be cool: hypey startup energy, gratuitous gradients, tech-bro minimalism

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** The platform for accessibility professionals must itself be deeply, thoughtfully accessible — not as a legal checkbox but as a demonstration of the community's values.
2. **Specific beats broad.** This is not for everyone. The design should feel like it was made for this exact community, which means it will feel delightfully strange to outsiders.
3. **Show, don't explain.** The personality should be legible before a single word is read. If the vibe needs a paragraph to explain it, the design isn't doing its job.
4. **Joy is serious work.** Playfulness and rigor are not opposites here. The community does hard, skilled, important work — the brand can be joyful AND credible.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA is the floor; WCAG 2.2 AA is the target. The audience includes disabled people — screen reader users, low-vision users, motor-disability users — and the platform is run by and for people whose professional lives are about access. There is no acceptable shortcut here. Accessible design is brand expression, not compliance overhead.
